# THE STOP — script & scene plan

60s · 16:9 · modern manifestation

## The angle

Manifestation content usually gets attacked as fake. That's the boring take, and it's
also wrong — attention genuinely does shape outcome. The sharper, more uncomfortable
argument: **it works, and that's precisely why it's being sold to you.**

The counterfeit inverts the mechanism. Real manifestation is attention that *costs*
something. The product version is attention that costs nothing and feels like
everything — and the feeling of almost-arriving is more addictive than arriving, so
the feed is optimized to keep you permanently almost there. He isn't manifesting.
He's the raw material something else is manifesting through.

The turn isn't "stop dreaming." It's: a wish and a plan differ by exactly one thing,
a price. Name it and the wish becomes a life.

The bus stop carries the whole argument and it was already in the supplied image —
you can visualize the bus all day, it still only stops if you're standing there.
Waiting is itself a cost: boredom, time, showing up.

## Structural notes

**The shake is the argument.** Camera shake ramps continuously from S5 to S8 and cuts
to dead-still on S10. The viewer feels the resolution before they parse the line.

**Restraint on the type.** Giant kinetic type only fires on S1, S4 and S11 — the
claim, the reversal, the landing. Used everywhere it would read as a template.

**Color is a single event.** The film is monochrome for 48 seconds so that one accent
at S10 does real work. This is the Phoenix Dialectic insertion point — a one-token
change once the design system is readable.
